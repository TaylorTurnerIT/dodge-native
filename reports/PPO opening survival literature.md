# Protect opening gains while preserving survival

The progressive1000 pilot shows **a tangible but inconsistent opening benefit**, not a reliable overall win: opening-1000 success averaged 70.38% in controls and 72.30% in curriculum policies (+1.92 percentage points), with replica changes of +6.54, +0.78, and −1.56 points. Across the same three pairs, mean survival changed +145.69, −140.79, and −32.60 frames; Q4 changed +22.95, −346.41, and −217.46. Thus two pairs had lower Q4, while the pooled Q4 difference was −180.31. Seed-conditional intervals do not measure training-run uncertainty; three pairs cannot establish a method effect. No curriculum replica advanced beyond the 1,000-frame stage, so this tested a short-stage/full-game mixture—not a completed progressive curriculum.

## 70% episode assignment did not guarantee 70% decision exposure

Curriculum research supports task sequencing or adaptive distributions, not an opening-gain/late-tail-loss prediction for this PPO game. Adaptive curricula helped in tested environments, but do not establish a useful fixed ratio here ([Narvekar et al., 2020](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/v21/20-212.html); [Klink et al., 2020](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/68a9750337a418a86fe06c1991a1d64c-Abstract.html)). Since short-stage episodes stop at 1,000 frames while full games can continue much longer, 70/30 describes episode assignments, not decisions, frames, or gradient contribution. Less late-state practice or shared-parameter interference could fit Q4; neither cause is established. Age-binned exposure counts are absent; terminal update-60 evaluation cannot reveal earlier improvement or regression.

## Artificial cutoffs and natural death train different targets

An imposed practice horizon is a truncation of the continuing game: bootstrap from its end-state value. Normal enemy death is an environment terminal. Time-limit research shows external cutoffs treated as terminal bias value targets. Bootstrap avoids that error but cannot supply omitted late-state action samples ([Pardo et al., 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/pardo18a.html)). The experiment design says artificial cutoffs bootstrap and normal deaths remain common. Nothing here establishes a cutoff-handling bug or makes one the cause of lower Q4.

## Three next steps, ranked for review

**1. Full episodes with capped opening-seed priority.** Keep normal-start trajectories running to death or the existing 18,000-frame cap. Oversample a fixed, training-only distribution of opening seed strata, retain a uniform sampling floor, refresh priorities from fresh current-policy rollouts, and cap per-seed share. Prioritized Level Replay selects training levels by learning potential and reports benefits in its tested procedurally generated tasks; applying that idea to fixed Dodge seeds is a project hypothesis, not a proven remedy ([Jiang et al., 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/jiang21b.html)). Test one predeclared priority mixture against uniform full episodes, matching parent, reward, architecture, LR, budget, and three seed vectors. Track opening success, age-binned survival/exposure, Q1/Q4, and fresh-reserved-seed mean. Risk: noisy priorities can overfit seeds or starve coverage.

**2. Mastery-gated adaptive mixture with a full-game floor.** Keep a measured minimum full-game decision/frame exposure floor; add short-stage practice only while stage mastery on a separate development seed cohort is below a preregistered threshold, advancing after disjoint windows. Never reuse final evaluation seeds for this gate. Compare one adaptive rule with fixed 70/30 at matched experience budget and seed vectors; log age-binned decisions. Frontier curricula adapt challenge to capability, but reverse-curriculum and regret-based environment studies use different task structures and do not prove this rule for Dodge ([Florensa et al., 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/florensa17a.html); [Dennis et al., 2020](https://research.google/pubs/emergent-complexity-and-zero-shot-transfer-via-unsupervised-environment-design/)). Risk: noisy gates and shifting exposure complicate interpretation.

**3. Isolated PPO step-size comparison, after diagnostics.** If logged KL, clip fraction, and stopped epochs show a plausible update-size issue, compare constant 3e-5 with one predeclared LR schedule. PPO benchmark studies show sensitivity and interactions, not the best Dodge LR ([Andrychowicz et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.05990)). Prior Dodge LR results are confounded by seed, duration, or best-of-N selection, so mechanism remains uncertain. Change only the schedule; hold parent, seed vectors, experience budget, other optimizer settings, and fresh final evaluation cohort constant. Keep epochs fixed. If later comparing epoch counts, account for optimizer-step/compute budget separately from equal experience. Risk: a schedule effect may vary across training seeds.

## Diagnose before changing optimization or reward

Offline, reconstruct age-binned decision/frame exposure, mastery windows, and cutoff/death masks. Pair terminal outcomes with logged KL, clip fraction, stopped epochs, losses, explained variance, and weights. These can associate outcomes with update behavior, not establish unsampled intermediate performance. PPO implementation choices and learning rate interact across benchmarks, so the existing data do not identify an optimizer cause ([Andrychowicz et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.05990); [Engstrom et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12729)). Keep reward ablation separate: survival-only could improve survival but lower score; score pursuit is not shown to cause early deaths. CVaR shifts toward the lower tail and needs an agreed mean/tail tradeoff. Age-weighted minibatches are a lower-priority explicit objective change: they alter gradient contributions by game age and are not exposure-only. Critic isolation/reset, more capacity, recurrent memory, and Adam reset also rank lower: EV/gradient diagnostics do not prove interference, and optimizer-reset evidence does not establish an Adam-reset PPO fix.

**Recommendation:** review the full-episode seed-priority experiment first, preceded by offline exposure and seed-level opening diagnostics. The training hold remains in place pending user review.
