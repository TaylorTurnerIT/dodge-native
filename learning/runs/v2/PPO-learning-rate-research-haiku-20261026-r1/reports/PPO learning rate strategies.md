# Measure PPO learning rates with paired runs

PPO learning rate (LR) matters because it interacts with Adam, minibatch size, and repeated epochs to determine how far policy and value parameters move. Published evidence supports tuning LR and shows that linear decay can help in some tasks, but it does not establish annealing or KL adaptation as universally superior. Dodge has not yet tested a schedule: its historical 3e-4 → 1e-4 → 3e-5 runs differ in seed and training duration, so they cannot identify an LR effect; the latest +8.69% came from more training at the same 3e-5. The next useful experiment is a preregistered, paired comparison of constant 3e-5 against a 60-update linear decay from 3e-5 to 1e-5, starting from the same accepted update-324 checkpoint. Treat this as a conditional comparison, repeat across seeds when budget allows, and keep an independent fixed endpoint.

## PPO makes LR consequential, but clipping does not cap each update

PPO clipping removes incentive to improve the surrogate by pushing probability ratios farther outside the clip interval; it is not a hard trust-region constraint. Engstrom et al. observed ratio violations in PPO while finding mean KL more constrained than in their unclipped variants, and described optimizer dependence as a source of sensitivity to LR and momentum. Adam’s updates also depend on moment estimates and epsilon, so LR is not literally a multiplier on a raw gradient followed by a fixed number of steps. Compare rates while matching initial optimizer state, training seeds, sample budgets, minibatch settings, configured epochs, and stopping rules; record realized KL and clip fraction. ([Schulman et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06347); [Engstrom et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.12729))

The same care applies to KL controls. PPO’s adaptive KL penalty changes the coefficient on a KL term in the objective; target-KL early stopping skips later epochs after measured divergence crosses a threshold; KL-adaptive LR changes the optimizer’s step size. These are distinct interventions. Dodge’s target-KL check is an epoch-level stop, not a hard constraint on each minibatch update. Neither clipping nor early stopping guarantees equal update counts across LR arms, and forcing equal realized optimizer steps would remove part of the treatment effect. ([Schulman et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06347); [Stable-Baselines3 PPO source snapshot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DLR-RM/stable-baselines3/master/stable_baselines3/ppo/ppo.py))

## Schedule evidence is mixed and specific to each benchmark

Schulman et al. report fixed Adam step size 3×10⁻⁴ for MuJoCo, KL-adjusted stepsize for Roboschool, and linear annealing of both LR and clip coefficient in Atari. Clipping outperformed tested KL-penalty variants in one comparison; these settings do not isolate a general schedule advantage. Engstrom et al. found Adam annealing among code-level optimizations needed to attain the highest reward within their grid on Humanoid-v2 and Walker2d-v2. This supports those tasks, not a universal ranking. ([Schulman et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06347); [Engstrom et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.12729))

The larger study by Andrychowicz et al. says LR strongly influences performance and that it should be tuned with interacting choices such as minibatch size. Their linear decay increased performance on four of five continuous-control tasks, but gains were described as small except for Ant; the authors call decay secondary to using Adam with a tuned LR. This supports a modest, controlled Dodge test, not a presumption that decay wins. CleanRL’s captured source uses `frac = 1 - (iteration - 1) / num_iterations`: its final iteration therefore uses `initial_lr / num_iterations`, not zero. SB3 updates its schedule from environment-timestep progress before training, illustrating that implementations differ in schedule denominator and timing. These repository snapshots were fetched from unpinned `main`/`master` branches. ([Andrychowicz et al., 2020, arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05990); [CleanRL continuous PPO source snapshot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vwxyzjn/cleanrl/master/cleanrl/ppo_continuous_action.py); [SB3 on-policy source snapshot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DLR-RM/stable-baselines3/master/stable_baselines3/common/on_policy_algorithm.py))

KL-driven LR rules exist in rsl_rl and rl-games source, but their defaults are implementation choices, not evidence of superiority over fixed LR. Retrieved rsl_rl material did not fully establish the exact adaptation frequency; rl-games’ captured code updates its scheduler using epoch-averaged KL. The review found no PPO-specific primary evidence for cosine decay or warmup; that means none was found in this limited review, not that none exists. ([rsl_rl PPO source snapshot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leggedrobotics/rsl_rl/main/rsl_rl/algorithms/ppo.py); [rl-games scheduler source snapshot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Denys88/rl_games/master/rl_games/common/schedulers.py))
